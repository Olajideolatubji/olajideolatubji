# Guilty Paws — Growth Playbook

Honest framing (user rule: never promise virality): YouTube outcomes are
probabilistic. Quality and consistency buy more lottery tickets; they do not
guarantee a winner. What this playbook guarantees is that **no video fails for
a reason we could have controlled**: weak hook, broken metadata, inconsistent
brand, or policy trouble.

## The patience protocol (user rule — canon)

- **Under 15 uploads:** low views are NORMAL. Ellis advises patience and keeps
  executing the format. No format changes, no panic pivots, no reading tea
  leaves in 3-view differences.
- **After 15 uploads, if no video has passed 50 views:** Ellis proposes a
  format change. Leading candidate (user's suggestion): **add a second
  recurring pet character** (foil/accomplice — e.g. a deadpan cat who watches
  the chaos, or a second dog who snitches). Other levers to consider at that
  point: length test, first-shot hook variants, title style test.
- A single video over 50 views resets the clock: double down on whatever it
  did (lane, disaster type, sound) with a 48h sequel.

## Phase roadmap

### Phase 0 — Foundation (now)
**Goal: eliminate every self-inflicted handicap. All free actions.**
- [ ] Fix the 5 launch titles in Studio (corrections in `logs/upload-log.md`).
- [ ] Paste the channel description from `brand-bible.md`; set country + channel keywords.
- [ ] Confirm **Altered content** disclosure is ticked on all 5 existing uploads.
- [ ] Name the dog (user decision).
- [ ] Authorize the channel in vidIQ (unlocks retention analytics later);
      until then Ellis asks the user for Studio numbers.
- Cadence: 2/day, lanes rotating, impossible-environments weighted ~50%.

### Phase 1 — Signal hunting (uploads 6–15)
- Every upload logged with lane + disaster type; weekly review compares.
- Judge *relative* performance only (video vs. channel median) — absolute
  numbers stay small and that is expected.
- One deliberate experiment per week max, one variable at a time, logged.

### Phase 2 — Verdict point (upload 15)
- Nothing >50 views → format-change proposal per the patience protocol.
- Something >50 views → double down: more of the winning lane, sequels,
  numbered series, pinned-comment lore.

### Phase 3 — Compounding (first sustained traction → 1,000 subs)
- Community tab at 500 subs: "what should he destroy next" polls.
- Retire the weakest lane; promote viewer-suggested premises.

### Phase 4 — Monetization (YPP: 1,000 subs + Shorts-feed threshold)
- The compliance section below is what makes this phase possible.
- Real revenue at scale: brand deals (pet brands) more than Shorts ad-share.

## The Shorts algorithm, practically

1. Each Short gets a small test audience; swipe-rate in the first ~2 seconds
   decides the next, bigger test. Shot 1 opening mid-disaster is our answer.
2. **Average % watched is the dominant signal.** At 15s, a full watch is easy
   and a loop (>100%) is realistic — the guilty side-eye ending into the
   disaster-reveal opening is engineered for that.
3. Shorts distribution is bursty and delayed — videos can pop weeks later.
   Never delete or private a "flopped" Short.
4. Early likes/comments correlate with wider testing — the pinned lore comment
   exists to bait the first comment.
5. Channel-level topical identity matters: the channel is *only* one dog's
   disasters. No off-brand uploads, ever.

## Compliance (critical for an AI channel — do not skip)

- **Altered-content disclosure on every upload** (user rule + YouTube policy).
  It does not hurt distribution; skipping it risks strikes.
- **"Repetitious content" is the #1 YPP rejection risk for AI channels.**
  Defenses baked into our format: an authored three-act joke per video (not a
  looping clip), recurring characters, human-written titles/premises, and
  distinct disasters per episode. This is the difference between "AI slop"
  and an animated comedy series that uses AI as the animation tool.
- **No apparent animal distress** — synthetic or not, it gets restricted. The
  dog is always calm; the brand rule is also the policy shield.
- Audio: Kling-generated sound only; no copyrighted music.
